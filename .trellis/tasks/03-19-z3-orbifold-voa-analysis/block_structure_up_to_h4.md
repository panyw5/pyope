# Neutral block structure up to $h=4$

We restrict to the $\mathbb Z_3$ orbifold subalgebra, i.e. to sectors with $\epsilon$-charge $0 \pmod 3$.

## Strong-generator working hypothesis

Current evidence supports the strong generating set
$$
\{T, J, G, \bar G, W, \bar W\}.
$$

No new independent top generator has been found in the neutral sectors of weights $2,3,4$ after quotienting by descendants/composites.

## Weight $2$ neutral block

The key OPE is
$$
W(z)\bar W(w)
\sim
-\frac{6}{(z-w)^3}
+\frac{18J(w)}{(z-w)^2}
+\frac{P_2(w)}{z-w},
$$
with
$$
P_2=-9:\beta^2\gamma^2:+18:\partial\beta\,\gamma:.
$$

Using the basis
$$
N_{2a}=:\beta^2\gamma^2:,
\qquad
N_{2b}=:\partial\beta\,\gamma:,
\qquad
N_{2c}=:\beta\,\partial\gamma:,
\qquad
N_{2d}=:b\partial c:,
$$
the weight-$2$ neutral content generated by $W\times\bar W$ lies inside the span of these fields.

Previous direct superprimary search shows there is no nonzero isolated neutral $\mathcal N=2$ superprimary in this ansatz space.

## Weight $3$ neutral continuation

The weight-$3$ neutral sector is generated by derivatives and composites built from the weight-$2$ block and the $\mathcal N=2$ algebra, e.g.
$$
\partial^2 J,
\quad
\partial T,
\quad
:JJ:,
\quad
:JT:,
\quad
:G\bar G:,
\quad
\partial(W\bar W)_{\text{pole }1}
$$
and the free-field neutral basis at this weight.

Direct OPE-based search found no new nonzero isolated neutral top generator at $h=3$.

## Weight $4$ neutral continuation

The neutral weight-$4$ space is much larger, but after quotienting by the obvious old directions
$$
\partial^3 J,
\qquad
\partial^2 T,
\qquad
:JT:,
\qquad
:TT:,
\qquad
\partial\bigl((W\bar W)_{\text{pole }1}\bigr),
$$
and related descendant/composite directions, the quotient space vanishes.

So there is again no genuinely new neutral top generator at $h=4$.

## Structural interpretation

The data up to $h=4$ supports the picture that

- $W,\bar W$ are the extra top generators beyond the $\mathcal N=2$ algebra;
- $W\times\bar W$ does not produce a clean new family generated by a neutral superprimary;
- instead it generates a neutral mixed block whose higher-weight content is exhausted by descendants/composites of already known structures.

So the orbifold closure is better described as
$$
\text{finite top generators } + \text{ neutral mixed }\mathcal N=2\text{ block closure}
$$
rather than by continually adding new neutral monomials as strong generators.
