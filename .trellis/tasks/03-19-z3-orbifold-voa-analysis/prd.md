# Z3 Orbifold VOA Analysis

## Problem

We study the VOA defined in `demo/N_equals_three_algebra.ipynb` under the section
`# $U(1)$ $\mathcal{N} = 4$ theory + $\mathbb{Z}_3$ gauging`.

The VOA is realized inside a free-field system generated by
$$
b,\ \partial c,\ \beta,\ \gamma
$$
with a $\mathbb{Z}_3$ quotient/orbifold interpretation.

The immediate goal is to understand:

- what the correct strong generators are;
- whether naive free-field invariant generators really close under OPE;
- whether the correct presentation should be in terms of $\mathcal{N}=2$ superconformal families/blocks rather than isolated normal-ordered monomials.

## Current Status

### Confirmed $\mathcal{N}=2$ SCA subalgebra

We have identified a 2d $\mathcal{N}=2$ superconformal subalgebra generated by
$$
J = :\beta\gamma:,
\qquad
G = :b\gamma:,
\qquad
\bar G = :\beta\partial c:,
$$
$$
T = \frac12\bigl(:\partial\beta\,\gamma: - :\beta\,\partial\gamma:\bigr) - :b\partial c:.
$$

The key OPE is
$$
G(z)\bar G(w)
\sim
\frac{1}{(z-w)^3}
+ \frac{J(w)}{(z-w)^2}
+ \frac{\tfrac12\partial J(w)-T(w)}{z-w}.
$$

### Chiral / anti-chiral generators

The natural fields
$$
W = :\beta^3:,
\qquad
\bar W = :\gamma^3:
$$
behave as $\mathcal{N}=2$ chiral / anti-chiral generators.

We also confirmed that
$$
X^+ = :\beta^2 b:
= \frac13\{G,W\}_1,
\qquad
X^- = :\gamma^2\partial c:
= -\frac13\{\bar G,\bar W\}_1.
$$
So $X^\pm$ are naturally understood as super-descendants of $W,\bar W$ rather than automatically as independent top generators.

### Naive strong-generation picture becomes complicated

Direct free-field OPE closure using monomials led to additional fields such as
$$
Y^- = :\gamma\partial b: - :\partial\gamma\,b:,
\qquad
Y^+ = :\partial\beta\,\partial c: - :\beta\partial^2 c:,
$$
and combinations in the neutral weight-two sector such as
$$
Z = 2:b\partial c: + :\beta^2\gamma^2: - 4:\partial\beta\,\gamma:.
$$

However, the current understanding is that these should **not** immediately be interpreted as new strong generators. They more likely belong to $\mathcal{N}=2$ families/blocks generated in OPE, and the monomial basis obscures this structure.

### Family/block point of view

The key OPE
$$
W(z)\bar W(w)
\sim
-\frac{6}{(z-w)^3}
+ \frac{18J(w)}{(z-w)^2}
+ \frac{P_2(w)}{z-w}
$$
with
$$
P_2 = -9:\beta^2\gamma^2: + 18:\partial\beta\,\gamma:
$$
suggests a decomposition of the form
$$
W \times \bar W \sim -6[\mathbf 1] + 18[J] + [\mathcal B],
$$
where $[\mathcal B]$ is a neutral $\mathcal{N}=2$ block generated from the weight-two sector rather than a clean single superprimary family.

### Weight-two neutral superprimary search

We systematically searched the neutral weight-two space spanned by
$$
:b\partial c:,
\quad
:\beta^2\gamma^2:,
\quad
:\beta\partial\gamma:,
\quad
:\partial\beta\,\gamma:
$$
for a nonzero $\mathcal{N}=2$ superconformal primary.

The generic candidate
$$
\Phi = a_1:b\partial c: + a_2:\beta^2\gamma^2: + a_3:\beta\partial\gamma: + a_4:\partial\beta\,\gamma:
$$
was constrained by the vanishing of higher OPE poles with $J,T,G,\bar G$.

The higher-pole constraints have a unique one-dimensional solution generated by
$$
\Phi_* = -2:b\partial c: - :\beta^2\gamma^2: + 2:\beta\partial\gamma: - 2:\partial\beta\,\gamma:.
$$

But this candidate still has nontrivial first-order poles with $G$ and $\bar G$, so it does not produce a clean independent $\mathcal{N}=2$ superprimary family. Current working conclusion:
$$
\text{there is no nonzero isolated neutral weight-two }\mathcal N=2\text{ superprimary in this ansatz space.}
$$

This strongly supports the interpretation that the neutral sector appearing in $W\times\bar W$ is an indecomposable/mixed $\mathcal N=2$ block rather than a single clean family.

## Working Hypothesis

The correct presentation of this VOA should likely be organized by $\mathcal N=2$ families/blocks, not by direct closure of free-field normal-ordered monomials.

In particular:

- $W,\bar W$ are top chiral / anti-chiral generators;
- $X^\pm$ are their super-descendants;
- the neutral sector in $W\times\bar W$ is best treated as a block $[\mathcal B]$ rather than a single primary family;
- some objects that looked like additional generators in monomial language are probably basis-dependent representatives of descendants or mixed block components.

## Proposed Plan

### 1. Classical / associated-graded counting

- Compute low-weight / low-charge Hilbert series or graded counting data.
- Determine expected dimensions of sectors with quantum numbers
  $$
  (h,q)=(1,0),\ (\tfrac32,\pm3),\ (2,\pm2),\ (2,0),\ (\tfrac52,\pm1),\dots
  $$
- Use this to predict how many independent families or relations should exist.

### 2. Sector-by-sector $\mathcal N=2$ primary search

- For each fixed $(h,q)$ sector, write the most general linear combination in the free-field basis.
- Solve the $\mathcal N=2$ superprimary conditions using `OPEdefs.m`.
- Record whether the sector contains:
  - a genuine superprimary;
  - only descendants;
  - or a nontrivial mixed block.

### 3. Build family/block graph

- Start from confirmed top generators such as $W,\bar W$.
- Apply the first-order OPE operations with $G,\bar G$ to generate descendants.
- Track how sectors are linked under $\mathcal N=2$ action.
- Define block notation such as
  $$
  [\mathbf 1],\ [J],\ [\mathcal B]
  $$
  whenever a clean single-family description is unavailable.

### 4. Rewrite key OPEs in block language

- First target:
  $$
  W\times\bar W.
  $$
- Then analyze
  $$
  W\times X^-,\qquad X^+\times\bar W,
  $$
  and determine whether they couple only to the same block $[\mathcal B]$ or to additional blocks.

### 5. Return to strong generators only after block structure is clear

- Once the block structure is understood, identify which top states must be chosen as actual strong generators.
- Avoid declaring every free-field monomial produced by OPE to be a new generator.

## Correction after further exploration

- When discussing the $\mathbb Z_3$ quotient/orbifold VOA, candidate strong generators must lie in the orbifold subalgebra, i.e. in sectors whose $\epsilon$-charge is $0 \pmod 3$.
- Therefore exploratory scans in sectors such as
  $$
  (h,q)=\left(\tfrac72,\pm1\right)
  $$
  are **not** directly relevant for deciding strong generators of the orbifold VOA.
- Those computations may still be useful as ambient free-field information, but they should not be used as evidence for or against new strong generators of the orbifold algebra itself.
- The correct next focus is to stay entirely inside the orbifold-allowed sectors and analyze closure/block structure there, especially the neutral sectors generated by OPEs of
  $$
  T,\ J,\ G,\ \bar G,\ W,\ \bar W.
  $$

## Current evidence after neutral-sector scans

- Inside the actual orbifold subalgebra, no new independent neutral top generator was found at weights $2,3,4$ after quotienting by the obvious descendants/composites.
- In particular, the neutral weight-$4$ quotient space also vanishes after modding out by old directions such as
  $$
  \partial^3 J,
  \qquad
  \partial^2 T,
  \qquad
  :JT:,
  \qquad
  :TT:,
  $$
  together with the natural descendants/composites coming from the $W\times \bar W$ neutral block.
- So the current best strong-generator candidate remains
  $$
  \{T,J,G,\bar G,W,\bar W\}.
  $$

## Main unresolved issue

- We do **not** yet have a global proof that
  $$
  \{T,J,G,\bar G,W,\bar W\}
  $$
  strongly generates the full $\mathbb Z_3$ quotient VOA.
- What we do have is strong low-weight evidence and a consistent block picture.
- The next session should therefore shift from low-weight scanning to a global generation argument.
